# 04 — Competitors & inspiration

## Direct (creator-led, high-protein / fakeaway)
| Brand | What works | Avoid | Idea to adapt |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fraser Reynolds — *Tasty High Protein* (Ebury) | Fakeaway chapter; calories and tracking barcodes per recipe | Publisher-site blandness | Make macros the hero of every recipe card |
| Meg Robinson @fitfoodiefeed — *Healthy in One* | Time-boxed recipes (under 30/45 min) | Generic cookbook retail page | Filter by cook time |
| Will Tennyson — *Bite Me* | Personality-first, "macro wins" framing | Over-busy YouTube styling | Lead with the creator's voice |
| TWT Recipe Vault (Payhip) | Bundles priced around A$40 | Payhip template look | Bundle anchoring |
| STRONGHERCLUB (Payhip) | MyFitnessPal barcodes | Template look | Future feature: barcodes |
| Stealth Health Cookbook | Category depth (wraps, quesadillas) | Long sales-letter page | Category tiles |

Sources: betterread.com.au, penguin.co.uk, allstora.com, payhip.com/b/F04Iq, payhip.com/STRONGHERCLUB, brandsearch.co (search 5 Nov 2026).

## Category best-in-class (from the earlier Mobbin research)
Cal AI, Lifesum, MyFitnessPal, MacroFactor, NYT Cooking, Kitchen Stories, Julienne. Shared traits: photo-led heroes, one CTA, macros as data visualisation, plan selectors.

## Motion / interaction references
Awwwards Food & Drink category, plus the Calo "Meal Plans Reimagined" Awwwards element (photography-led). The pattern worth adapting: pinned horizontal sections driven by vertical scroll, and big numerals that count up.

## Clichés to avoid
Neon-green gym gradients, a shirtless hero shot, "Transform your body" copy, a stock salad bowl, three identical feature cards, countdown-timer "sales" pressure.

## Opening this brand can claim
Every competitor says "healthy food that tastes good". Only Aussie Fitness has **a 60kg proof story built on fast-food swaps**. The site's job is to dramatise the swap: *the food you crave, with the macros you need*.
